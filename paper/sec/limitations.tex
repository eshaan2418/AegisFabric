\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Emulation is not a WAN.}
Links are veth pairs with \texttt{tbf}/\texttt{netem} inside one Linux VM, with capacities scaled down
so that a CPU testbed sits in a communication-bound regime. Real WANs have queueing, cross traffic,
loss processes and failure modes (partial failures, gray links, asymmetric congestion) that our scripted
failures only approximate. The \texttt{speed} signal reads the configured shaper rate. That is realistic
for a carrier that lowers a circuit's committed rate and reports it, but it makes bandwidth collapses
directly observable to the controller. The ablation without \texttt{speed} measures how much the results
depend on that.

\paragraph{CPUs are not GPUs.}
There is no accelerator. Compute per step is that of a tiny model on a few CPU threads, and
the ratio of compute to communication was chosen, then measured and reported, rather than inherited from a
real cluster. A GPU cluster at the same WAN capacities would be even more communication-bound; at datacenter
interconnect speeds most of the effects reported here would vanish.

\paragraph{Small search space.}
Six ranks on three sites allow exhaustive search. A production controller would need ILP/LNS or learned
policies, plus explicit treatment of tensor/pipeline parallelism, which our CPU rung only exercises in
M1 (FSDP2/HSDP). The joint planner's advantage depends on its cost model. The model is calibrated per
plane and refined online, but it remains a fluid approximation of TCP over shaped links.

\paragraph{Shared host.}
Other workloads ran on the host throughout (load averages are recorded per run). Methods were interleaved
in a seeded random order within each (seed, scenario) block, so slow drift affects them alike, but it
inflates variance. Absolute step times should not be compared across tables collected at different times.

\paragraph{Routing granularity.}
Without Open vSwitch, traffic engineering acts on (destination prefix, class) through BGP
local-preference, not per flow. This matches how many enterprises would steer traffic, but finer-grained
control could do better for both the baselines and the joint planner.

\paragraph{Security and privacy scope.}
Encryption and authentication of checkpoints protect confidentiality and integrity against the storage
service. They are not privacy: models trained on confidential data can leak it, which is why we
measure membership inference separately (\S\ref{sec:ext}). SPIRE attestation is not deployed. The
anti-rollback high-water mark is held by clients, not by a trusted controller. DP results are for a toy
model with tiny private sets and say nothing about foundation-model-scale DP.

\paragraph{Storage.}
Filesystems are loop-mounted images on a VM virtual disk backed by a laptop SSD. fsync durability is only
as strong as that stack's flush semantics, and \texttt{kill -9} does not model power loss.

\paragraph{What a real-scale test needs.}
Real multi-site GPU capacity with a programmable WAN (or a carrier test circuit), production-size
models with tensor/pipeline parallelism, failure traces from an operational network rather than scripted
injections, and a TE system representative of what the network team actually runs.
