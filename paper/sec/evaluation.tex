\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\paragraph{Workload.}
All runs train the \texttt{small} TinyVLM preset with \cfg{6} ranks, a per-rank batch of \cfg{16} and
\cfg{2} threads per rank. Every lab campaign ran from a frozen checkout at a single commit
(\texttt{bench/lab\_campaigns.sh}). Means are reported with 95\% confidence half-widths over seeds.

\subsection{Baselines: parallelism over the WAN (M1)}
\label{sec:eval-m1}
Table~\ref{tab:m1} compares DDP, FSDP2 and HSDP (shard within a site, replicate across sites) on a single
namespace (``loopback'', no WAN) and with two ranks per site on the nominal emulated WAN under static
routing, over \CmOneSeeds{} seeds. Every run reached the target evaluation loss. On loopback the three modes
are within noise of each other and collectives are a small share of the step. On the WAN, a DDP step takes
\CmOneWanOverLoDdp$\times$ as long as on loopback, and collectives account for \CmOneWanDdpColl\% of it, so
this testbed sits in the communication-bound regime the study needs. FSDP2 is the slowest over the WAN,
because its per-layer all-gathers and reduce-scatters all cross sites. HSDP keeps sharding traffic inside
a site and matches DDP. We use DDP for the controller study.

\subsection{Network laboratory: convergence per failure type (M2)}
\label{sec:eval-m2}
A \cfg{5}\,ms UDP prober runs between compute hosts in Toronto and Montr\'eal over the plane-A path, and
each failure is injected on that link (\CmTwoTrials{} trials per type, BGP keepalive/hold \cfg{1}/\cfg{3}\,s).
A carrier loss (\emph{link down}) is detected locally: the FIB moves to the surviving path in
\CmTwoLinkDownFib\,s and the data-plane outage is \CmTwoLinkDownOutage\,s. A \emph{blackhole} keeps the carrier
up while dropping everything, so it is detected only when the BGP hold timer expires, and the outage grows
to \CmTwoBlackholeOutage{}$\pm$\CmTwoBlackholeOutageCi\,s. A \emph{BGP session reset} on the link costs a
\CmTwoBgpResetOutage\,s outage while the route is withdrawn and re-learned, and the session needs
\CmTwoBgpResetBgpReest{}$\pm$\CmTwoBgpResetBgpReestCi\,s to re-establish. \emph{Flaps} behave like repeated link
downs (\CmTwoFlapOutage\,s per event). BGP does not react to the two failures that degrade a link without
breaking it. A bandwidth collapse cuts TCP throughput from \CmTwoBwCollapseTputBefore\ to
\CmTwoBwCollapseTputDuring\,Mbit/s at unchanged RTT, and a latency spike raises RTT from
\CmTwoLatSpikeRttBefore\ to \CmTwoLatSpikeRttDuring\,ms. Routing \emph{reachability} therefore recovers within
about a hold time even for silent failures, and sub-second when the carrier drops. Performance failures are invisible to BGP, and they are where a
controller that knows the job can help (\S\ref{sec:eval-m3}). The training step-time impact of each
failure under static routing is the \textsf{static} column of the M3 results.
