# M8 notes: post-training, DP-SGD + membership inference, federated fine-tuning

Scope: the "smaller-scope extension" of the spec. Everything runs on the CPU/MPS rung of this host (B-001), uses
synthetic data only, and is tiny (848,471-parameter char transformer). Every number below is quoted from a
result file written via `bench.meta.write_result`; the file is named next to each table. `results/m8/REPORT.md`
(generated by `uv run python -m bench.m8_report`) renders the same tables directly from the summaries.

Provenance: all result files were written from clean trees (`git.dirty = false`) at commits 77aa983 … b0c78a3
on branch `worktree-agent-a4a85e3e785df7874`. Between those commits only driver options, plotting, the report,
the clip-only mode and the v2 shadow/rescoring code were added; the training/eval code paths used by earlier
files were not changed (see `git log -- posttrain privacy data/synth`). Partial results from the earlier

## Task, model, compute rule

- **Task** (`data/synth/qa.py`, built on `data/synth/finance.py` statements): a question about a hidden
  synthetic statement (category total, count, closing balance, largest charge by merchant or by amount, and
  the difference between two categories), with paraphrase templates and category synonyms. The policy must emit
  tool calls (`total`, `count`, `closing`, `largest`, `calc`). The environment inserts `R: <result>` lines,
  and the policy ends with `A: <answer>`. **Exact match (EM)** means the final answer string equals gold.
  **Tool-call success (tool_ok)** means at least one call was made and every call line parsed and executed.
  Held-out eval: 500 episodes (seed 777); dev split for LR tuning (seed 998); training splits use disjoint seeds.
  No real PII: names and account numbers come from fixed fictional vocabularies plus random digits (`SYN-…`).
- **Model** (`posttrain/model.py`): our own char-level causal transformer (4 layers, d=128, 4 heads, 848,471
  params), with KV-cached batched decoding that interleaves forced tool-result tokens. It is built only from
  Embedding/Linear/LayerNorm, so Opacus per-sample gradients work.
- **Warm start** (per seed, `posttrain.task.warm_start`): 150 LM steps on public synthetic statements and
  transcripts, then 200 SFT steps on 2000 gold episodes (batch 64), costing 1.86e13 FLOPs. Warm-start EM is
  0.322 [0.313, 0.330]. The warm start solves `count` and `largest_m`, scores ≤ 0.004 on `total`, `closing` and
  `diff`, and 0.01 on `largest_a` (`results/m8/posttrain/summary.json`, by_type).
- **Compute-matching rule**: every method gets the same budget of *analytic FLOPs after the warm start*.
  That is 2N per token for every forward-only pass (sampling, reference-model log-probs, RM scoring) and 6N
  per token for forward+backward, with N = all parameters and the attention term ignored (T ≤ 256). A method
  stops at the first step past the budget, so overshoot is at most one batch (FLOPs-used column). Token
  counts and step counts are in the per-seed files (`compute.train_tokens`, `compute.fwd_tokens`,
  `iters`/`rounds`). Wall-clock is reported but the host is shared (B-005), so it is only indicative.
  **Exception, stated:** the reward model's *training* compute (≈5e12 FLOPs per seed,
  `reward_model.rm_compute`) is **not** charged to GRPO-RM. Only RM scoring during GRPO is charged.

## 1. SFT vs DPO vs GRPO at equal compute (5 seeds, mean [95% CI])

Methods (`posttrain/methods.py`, our own implementations, tested in `tests/test_posttrain.py`):
- **SFT**: continued SFT on 4000 *additional gold demonstrations*. It is the only method with labels beyond the
  warm start.
- **DPO**: online/iterative. Each round samples 64 prompts × 4 completions (T=1) from the current policy.
  Pairs are best vs worst under the verifiable reward (EM + 0.1·tool_ok), with β=0.1 and a fixed warm-start
  reference.
- **GRPO**: 32 prompts × group 8, group-normalised advantages, one on-policy step per batch (so the
  clipped-ratio term reduces to the policy gradient), and k3 KL to the reference (β=0.02).
  **Verifiable reward**: EM + 0.1·tool_ok, from the answer/tool checker.
- **GRPO-RM**: the reward is a learned **reward model**'s P(correct) + 0.1·tool_ok. The RM is a scalar-head
  copy of the warm-start trunk, trained on 3000 warm-start rollouts labelled by EM and tested on 1000 held-out
  rollouts. Held-out accuracy 0.975 [0.965, 0.985], AUC 0.995 [0.992, 0.998], positive rate 0.252.
- LRs were picked on the dev split with tuning seed 100 from a 2-point grid per method
  (`results/m8/posttrain/tune.json`: sft 1e-3, dpo 3e-4, grpo 1e-4, grpo_rm = grpo's).

Budget 4e12 FLOPs (≈ 22% of the warm-start cost), `results/m8/posttrain/summary.json`:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| warm start | 0.322 [0.313, 0.330] | 0.887 [0.860, 0.913] | (1.86e13 to build) | 69 |
| SFT | 0.543 [0.297, 0.789] | 0.961 [0.935, 0.986] | 4.02e12 | 21 |
| DPO | 0.317 [0.300, 0.333] | 0.998 [0.996, 1.000] | 4.03e12 | 409 |
| GRPO | 0.322 [0.313, 0.332] | 0.975 [0.938, 1.013] | 4.05e12 | 259 |
| GRPO-RM | 0.316 [0.304, 0.328] | 0.945 [0.890, 1.000] | 4.09e12 | 156 |

At this budget SFT is bimodal across seeds (0.650, 0.326, 0.712, 0.698, 0.328 for seeds 0–4). Welch
SFT-vs-others p ≈ 0.06–0.07, uncorrected, so the difference is not significant at n=5.

Budget 1.6e13 FLOPs (4×), `results/m8/posttrain/budget1.6e13/summary.json`. Same warm starts; LRs reused from
the 4e12 tuning (not re-tuned):

| method | EM | tool-call success | FLOPs used | wall s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| warm start (retrained; see note) | 0.308 [0.293, 0.322] | 0.881 [0.860, 0.903] | (1.86e13 to build) | 53 |
| SFT | **0.865 [0.808, 0.922]** | 0.990 [0.982, 0.997] | 1.60e13 | 63 |
| DPO | 0.320 [0.311, 0.329] | 0.998 [0.996, 1.000] | 1.60e13 | 778 |
| GRPO | 0.323 [0.314, 0.332] | 0.997 [0.992, 1.001] | 1.60e13 | 543 |
| GRPO-RM | 0.294 [0.264, 0.323] | 0.713 [0.386, 1.040] | 1.60e13 | 268 |

SFT beats every other method (Welch p < 1e-4 for each; Hedges g ≈ −14 to −15). GRPO vs GRPO-RM: p = 0.048,
uncorrected. Note: the warm starts are rebuilt per run with the same seeds, but MPS kernels are not
bit-deterministic, so this run's warm start EM (0.308) differs slightly from the 4e12 run's (0.322).

**Reward hacking with the learned RM (measured):** in GRPO-RM the training reward (RM P(correct) + 0.1·tool_ok)
rises from ≈0.35 to 0.67–1.0 on every seed. True EM stays at or below the warm start (seed 4: 0.252), and
tool-call validity collapses on 3 of 5 seeds (0.454, 0.676, 0.466). Plain GRPO's training reward stays at
0.30–0.54, its tool validity stays ≥ 0.99, and its EM is unchanged. The RM is 97% accurate on warm-start
rollouts but is exploited off-distribution (`mean_train_reward_first/last`, `eval` in
`results/m8/posttrain/budget1.6e13/seed*.json`).

Findings (measured):
- At equal analytic compute, **SFT on gold demonstrations is the only method that raised EM**: 0.543 at 4e12
  (bimodal), 0.865 at 1.6e13. DPO, GRPO and GRPO-RM stayed at the warm-start EM at both budgets. They did move *tool-call validity* (DPO 0.887 → 0.998
  at 4e12).
- The per-type data are consistent with an exploration failure. The warm start scores 0 on
  `total`/`closing`/`diff`, and the reward-model data show only 25% positive samples, roughly the
  count/largest_m share. On-policy methods with an outcome reward therefore get almost no positive samples
  for the failing types. We did not measure per-type sample success directly; this is an interpretation.
- Under a FLOP-matched rule most RL compute goes to sampling. At 4e12, SFT back-propagated through ≈0.79M
  tokens. GRPO back-propagated through ≈0.38–0.40M tokens in 32–39 updates and spent ≈1.2M forward tokens on
  sampling. DPO back-propagated through ≈0.22M tokens and sampled ≈1.7M (`compute.train_tokens`,
  `compute.fwd_tokens`, `iters`/`rounds` in the seed files).
- Not a claim: anything about the relative merit of DPO/GRPO at scale, with a stronger warm start, with
  process rewards, or with partial-credit rewards.

## 2. DP-SGD (Opacus) and membership inference (5 seeds)

**Private fine-tuning step (the only DP step):** supervised fine-tuning of a *public task model* on
D_priv = 1200 private tool-use episodes + 100 member canaries. The public task model is LM pretraining + SFT on
public synthetic episodes, seed 0, EM 0.323 (`results/m8/privacy/base_eval.json`). DP is **example-level**: one
record = one episode or one canary. Hyperparameters: Poisson sampling q = 128/1300 = 0.0985, 6 epochs = 66
steps, clipping norm C = 1.0, δ = 1e-5, AdamW lr 1e-3 (DP-Adam). σ comes from
`make_private_with_epsilon`. **Accountant: RDP** (Opacus); PRV recomputed on the same (σ, q, steps) as a
cross-check. Non-private baseline: same batch/epochs/lr, no per-sample clipping, no noise (batch-level clip
1.0). **Clip-only ablation**: Opacus Poisson sampling + per-sample clipping with σ = 0, which is not private.

**Canaries / overfitting for a meaningful MIA**: `note SYN-dddddddd pin dddd-dddd` (16 random digits). A fixed
pool of 200 is split 50/50 per seed. Each member is inserted **once** into a deliberately small private set
(1300 records) trained for 6 epochs with no weight decay, so each high-entropy secret is seen 6 times. That is
enough for the non-private model to separate members (loss-threshold AUC 0.743). Attacks: loss threshold;
offline LiRA (K=3 OUT shadows per ε, per-canary or pooled variance); shadow-calibrated loss.

`results/m8/privacy/summary.json` (shadow-based columns from `results/m8/privacy/mia_v2/`):

(Per-canary-variance LiRA and shadow-calibrated AUCs are in the same summary and track the pooled-variance
column within ±0.015. Negative CI bounds come from the t-interval on 5 seeds of a bounded quantity.)

Findings (measured):
- **ε vs utility is flat**: every DP setting, including ε=1024, leaves EM at the public model's level (0.33),
  while non-private fine-tuning gains +0.18 EM. The clip-only ablation reaches 0.676, so per-sample clipping is
  not what destroys utility (it even helps here). The Gaussian noise does. Even σ=0.12 in an 848k-dim gradient
  has expected norm σ·C·√d/B ≈ 0.9 per step, against a clipped mean-gradient norm ≤ 1, and the step count is
  small (66). This is an interpretation from those quantities; it was not measured separately.
- **MIA rises with ε even where utility does not**: LiRA AUC goes from 0.52 (ε=1) to 0.67 (ε=1024), against
  0.76 non-private. At large ε the model leaks canary membership with no task gain, which is the worst
  trade-off.
- ε (PRV) ≤ ε (RDP) everywhere, as expected: RDP is the looser bound, much looser at large ε.
- **Bug caught and fixed**: the first (v1) shadows were trained without any canaries. Their canary loss
  (~7 nats) dwarfed even the targets' non-member losses (~1.2, since targets learn the canary *format*), so
  LiRA scores saturated and gave AUC 0.5 by construction. v2 shadows see 100 same-format reference canaries
  from a disjoint pool (asserted disjoint). They were rescored from the canary losses stored in each target
  file; v1 numbers remain in the seed files.

## 3. Federated fine-tuning simulation (3 sites tor/mtl/us, non-IID, 5 seeds)

`privacy/federated.py`, `bench/m8_federated.py`. The sites are in-process. Data are non-IID: site-skewed
merchant mixes and strongly different question-type mixes (TOR mostly totals/counts, MTL closing/diff, US
largest-charge), 600 episodes per site. All methods start from the public **LM-only** base (no tool-use
SFT), so every bit of task skill comes from site data. Equal work: 8 rounds × 10 local steps × 3 sites = 240
local steps at batch 64 for FedAvg/FedProx (μ=0.01); centralized gets 240 steps on the pooled data; each
local-only model gets 80 steps. Eval: 100 held-out episodes per site from its own distribution; "global" = mean
over sites. `results/m8/federated/summary.json`:

| method | global EM | TOR | MTL | US |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FedAvg | 0.148 [0.126, 0.170] | 0.154 | 0.042 | 0.248 |
| FedProx (μ=0.01) | 0.139 [0.111, 0.168] | 0.114 | 0.040 | 0.264 |
| centralized (pooled raw data) | 0.511 [0.475, 0.548] | 0.524 | 0.324 | 0.686 |
| local-only TOR | 0.127 [0.110, 0.143] | 0.254 | 0.024 | 0.102 |
| local-only MTL | 0.053 [0.042, 0.064] | 0.076 | 0.040 | 0.042 |
| local-only US | 0.167 [0.141, 0.194] | 0.102 | 0.054 | 0.346 |

Communication, accounted rather than measured on a wire (fp32 weights up and down for each of 3 sites per
round): 20.4 MB/round, 162.9 MB total for 8 rounds. Pooling the raw site episodes for centralized training
moves only 0.17 MB (seed 0, `central.comm.raw_data_bytes_pooled`).

Rounds × local-steps sweep at a fixed 80 local steps per site, FedAvg (`results/m8/federated/comm_sweep/`,
in `summary.json.comm_sweep`):

| rounds × local steps | global EM | total bytes |
|---|---|---|
| 80 × 1 | 0.075 [0.049, 0.100] | 1629 MB |
| 16 × 5 | 0.155 [0.137, 0.172] | 326 MB |
| 8 × 10 (main) | 0.148 [0.126, 0.170] | 163 MB |
| 4 × 20 | 0.119 [0.080, 0.157] | 81 MB |

Findings / practicality (honest):
- FedAvg reaches 29% of centralized EM (Welch p < 1e-4 vs centralized). Its global EM is even below the best
  local-only model (US, 0.167); it is better than local-US only on TOR. It moves ~1000× more bytes than
  shipping the raw data would. FedProx(0.01) does not help
  (p = 0.52 vs FedAvg).
- More frequent averaging is *not* better here: 80×1 is the worst. A plausible cause is the fresh AdamW
  optimiser per round (stateless clients), which makes 1-step rounds mostly bias-correction warm-up. This was
  not separately measured.
- For this toy, FL is impractical on bytes, accuracy and engineering. Its only argument is that raw data stay
  on site, and FL alone gives **no formal privacy**: updates can leak training data, and nothing here
  measures that. A real deployment would need secure aggregation, DP on client updates, and handling of
  stragglers and dropouts. None of that is simulated (no WAN, no failures).

## Limitations (read before quoting)
- Toy scale: an 848k-param char model and a templated task. Nothing transfers to foundation-model scale. Even
  the method ranking may change with model size, warm-start quality, reward design and budget.
- Compute matching is analytic (6N/2N, attention ignored), not measured FLOPs. RM training is not charged to
  GRPO-RM. LRs were tuned on a 2-point grid at 4e12 only and reused at 1.6e13.
- DP: the guarantee is example-level and covers this fine-tuning step only. It does not cover the public
  pretraining/SFT, hyperparameter selection (done on non-private dev data and assumed public), or several
  records from one customer (group privacy multiplies ε). Opacus `secure_mode` was off. **DP on a toy model is
  not a DP guarantee for any foundation model**, and the utility collapse here is specific to the small private
  set (1300 records) and short schedule. Larger n/batch would raise the signal-to-noise ratio and was not
  explored.
- MIA uses only 3 OUT shadows (offline LiRA); online LiRA with IN/OUT shadows would be stronger. AUCs are
  lower bounds on leakage for this setup.
- **Encryption ≠ privacy**: M5 mTLS/envelope encryption protects checkpoints and channels, but the trained
  weights can still leak training data through their outputs. That is what the MIA measures, and only DP
  bounds it.
- Federated: in-process simulation with communication *accounted*, not measured. 3 sites, no secure
  aggregation, no DP on updates, no stragglers.
- Host shared with a concurrent timing-sensitive experiment: all runs used `nice -n 19`, ≤ 4 torch threads per
  process (2–4) and MPS. Wall-clock numbers are indicative only. MPS kernels are not bit-deterministic, so
  reruns reproduce the statistics, not bit-identical numbers.

## Proposed DECISIONS entries
- **D-011 (2026-10-24) M8 post-training comparison rule.** Methods are compared at equal *analytic*
  post-warm-start FLOPs: 2N per forward token (sampling, reference, RM scoring) and 6N per fwd+bwd token, every
  method from the same per-seed warm start. RM training is a separate, reported cost. Own implementations of
  SFT/iterative DPO/GRPO/RM (TRL not used: tool-interleaved char model, offline mirror). Verifiable reward =
  EM + 0.1·tool_ok.
- **D-012 (2026-10-24) M8 DP step and MIA design.** DP-SGD (Opacus, RDP accountant, PRV cross-check, Poisson
  sampling, C=1, δ=1e-5) is applied only to the private supervised fine-tuning step, at example level.
  Membership is measured on 16-digit random canaries inserted once. Offline LiRA shadows must contain
  same-format reference canaries from a disjoint pool (v1 without them saturated). Clip-only (σ=0) is
  reported as an ablation to separate clipping from noise.
- **D-013 (2026-10-24) M8 federated simulation.** In-process FedAvg/FedProx over 3 non-IID sites with
  communication accounted as fp32 weight bytes. Compared with centralized and local-only at equal total local
  steps, plus a rounds × local-steps sweep at fixed local work.
